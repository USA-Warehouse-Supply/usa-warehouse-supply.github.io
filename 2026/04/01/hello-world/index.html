How to Choose the Right Stretch Wrap for Your Warehouse

As a warehouse operator in the US, you know that stretch wrap is the backbone of secure palletizing. It’s not just about wrapping pallets—it’s about protecting your inventory from damage during transit, reducing load shifts, and staying efficient in fast-paced distribution centers.

With endless options in thickness, width, and application methods, choosing the right stretch wrap can feel daunting—especially when you need to align with US industry standards and operational needs.

This guide breaks down everything you need to select stretch wrap. We’ll cover critical specs, compare hand vs. machine-applied films, and provide actionable tips tailored to US storage and shipping scenarios.

Key Specifications to Master

Before choosing a stretch wrap, you need to understand the specs that directly impact performance, cost, and ease of use. Here’s what matters most for US warehouses:

1. Thickness (Mils)

Thickness is measured in mils (1 mil = 0.001 inches) and determines the film’s strength, puncture resistance, and durability. Contrary to popular belief, thicker isn’t always better—modern high-performance films deliver superior strength at thinner gauges, saving you money on material usage.

Thickness Range (Mils) Common Use Case
0.6–0.9 mil Light-duty loads (≤1,100 lbs), uniform cartons, short-term indoor storage (e.g., e-commerce fulfillment centers)
0.9–1.2 mil Medium-duty loads (1,100–3,300 lbs), mixed SKUs, regular warehouse storage (the most versatile option for US facilities)
1.2–2.0 mil Heavy-duty loads (>3,300 lbs), sharp edges (e.g., metal parts, lumber), long-term outdoor storage (exposed to weather)

Pro Tip: For most US warehouses, a 1.0 mil LLDPE film offers the best balance of strength and cost-efficiency. It works for 80% of standard pallet loads and uses 20–30% less material than a 1.5 mil low-quality film.

2. Width (Inches)

Width is tied to your pallet size and application method—US warehouses primarily use these standard widths:

  • Hand wrap: 12-inch, 14-inch, or 18-inch rolls (flexible for small pallets, irregular items, or spot-wrapping)

  • Machine wrap: 20-inch rolls (matches the width of standard 48x40-inch pallets and US wrapping machines)

  • Narrow widths (4–8 inches): Ideal for bundling small items (e.g., tools, tubes) or reinforcing load tops/bases .

3. Stretch Ratio

Stretch ratio refers to how much the film can elongate before breaking (e.g., 300% stretch = 1 foot stretches to 3 feet). Higher ratios mean more coverage per roll, tighter load tension, and lower cost per pallet—critical for US warehouses handling high volumes.

  • Hand wrap: 200–400% stretch (depends on manual effort; most workers achieve 250–300% in practice)

  • Machine wrap: 300–500% stretch (automated pre-stretching ensures consistent performance)

  • Pre-stretched hand wrap: Factory-stretched to 80% of maximum capacity (reduces manual effort by 50%—a game-changer for busy US warehouses) .

4. Material Type

US warehouses rely on three primary polyethylene materials, each with distinct benefits:

  • LLDPE (Linear Low-Density Polyethylene): The industry standard. Offers excellent stretchability, puncture resistance, and cling—perfect for mixed loads, irregular shapes, and standard US pallets.

  • LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene): More affordable and flexible but less durable. Suitable for light, non-abrasive items (e.g., plastic containers, apparel boxes).

  • HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene): Rigid and tear-resistant with minimal stretch. Used for heavy, stable loads (e.g., drums, concrete blocks) or as a reinforcing layer .

5. Cling

Cling is the film’s ability to stick to itself (not your products) and is critical for load stability. US warehouses prefer:

  • Single-sided cling: Prevents film from sticking to pallets, lift trucks, or conveyor belts (cleaner application and less downtime).

  • Double-sided cling: Ideal for lightweight or uneven loads (e.g., empty boxes, fragile items) that need extra adhesion .

Avoid films with excessive cling—they can cause jams in automated systems or damage printed packaging (common in retail-focused US warehouses).

6. Color

  • Clear: Most popular in US facilities—allows barcode scanning and easy inventory checks (essential for WMS systems).

  • Black/Colored: Blocks UV rays (critical for outdoor storage in states like Florida or Texas) and deters theft of high-value goods.

  • Tinted: Balances UV protection and visibility (great for long-term indoor storage with natural light) .

Hand Wrap vs. Machine Wrap: US Warehouse Comparison

The choice between hand and machine-applied wrap depends on your warehouse volume, pallet count, and labor costs—key considerations for US businesses. Here’s a side-by-side breakdown:

Feature Hand Stretch Wrap Machine Stretch Wrap
Application Speed 1–2 pallets per minute 3–5 pallets per minute
Labor Requirement High (manual effort; risk of worker fatigue) Low (automated; frees staff for other tasks)
Stretch Ratio 200–400% (inconsistent) 300–500% (consistent)
Roll Size Small (5–10 lbs) Large (30–45 lbs)
Cost Per Pallet $0.75–$1.25 (higher material/labor costs) $0.30–$0.60 (lower material usage)
Best For Low volume (≤10 pallets/day), irregular loads, small US warehouses High volume (≥10 pallets/day), standard 48x40-inch pallets, distribution centers

Key Insight: For US warehouses handling 20+ pallets daily, a semi-automatic wrapping machine (starting at ~$3,000) typically pays for itself in 6–8 months through labor and material savings .

How to Select the Right Stretch Wrap for US Warehouses

Use this scenario-based guide to match stretch wrap to your specific needs—tailored to common US warehouse operations:

By Load Type & Weight (US Pounds)

  • Lightweight/Uniform (≤1,100 lbs): 0.7–0.9 mil LLDPE hand wrap (e.g., e-commerce boxes, small parts). Works for USPS or FedEx shipments.

  • Medium/Irregular (1,100–3,300 lbs): 1.0–1.2 mil pre-stretched LLDPE (hand or machine). Ideal for mixed retail SKUs or grocery distribution.

  • Heavy/Dense (>3,300 lbs): 1.5–2.0 mil machine-applied LLDPE (e.g., industrial parts, building materials). Meets US trucking safety standards for load security.

  • Sharp Edges: 1.2–1.5 mil blown LLDPE (thicker, tougher film) or reinforced film with metallocene additives (prevents tears from metal corners or lumber) .

By Storage Environment (US Climate Considerations)

  • Indoor/Short-Term (≤3 months): Clear 0.9–1.0 mil LLDPE (cost-effective for temperature-controlled warehouses common in the US).

  • Indoor/Long-Term (>3 months): 1.0–1.2 mil film with strong cling (prevents load settling in high-rack storage).

  • Outdoor/Exposed: Black 1.5–2.0 mil UV-resistant LLDPE (protects against sun, rain, and temperature swings—critical for warehouses in the South or Midwest).

  • Humid/Refrigerated: Anti-fog film (prevents condensation buildup in cold storage facilities, common in food and pharmaceutical US warehouses) .

By Cost Efficiency (US Budget Focus)

  • Budget-Conscious: 0.9 mil LLDPE hand wrap (balances performance and price for small US warehouses).

  • High-Volume Operations: Machine-applied pre-stretched film (reduces material usage by 40% vs. hand wrap).

  • Premium Protection: True-gauge film (consistent thickness and stretch—worth the investment for high-value inventory like electronics or medical supplies) .

Quick Reference Selection Table (US Units)

Scenario Recommended Film Key Specs (US Units)
E-commerce fulfillment (hand-wrapped) LLDPE hand wrap 0.8 mil, 14-inch width, 300% stretch
Retail distribution (machine-wrapped) Pre-stretched LLDPE 1.0 mil, 20-inch width, 400% stretch
Industrial parts (sharp edges) Blown LLDPE 1.5 mil, 20-inch width, high puncture resistance
Outdoor storage (Texas/Florida) UV-resistant LLDPE 1.8 mil, black, 20-inch width
Cold storage (food/pharma) Anti-fog LLDPE 1.0 mil, clear, 18-inch width

Final Tips for US Warehouse Success

  1. Test Before Buying: Order sample rolls to test on your most common loads (e.g., 48x40-inch pallets with mixed retail SKUs). Check for stretchability, cling, and compatibility with your equipment.

  2. Calculate Total Cost of Ownership: Don’t just compare roll prices—factor in labor costs (hand vs. machine) and material usage. A $50 roll of pre-stretched film may cover 50 pallets, while a $30 roll of low-quality film covers only 20.

  3. Train Your Team: Ensure staff follow proper application techniques (e.g., 4–6 wraps at the base, 2–3 at the top) to meet US DOT load security standards for truck shipping.

  4. Prioritize Sustainability: US customers and regulators increasingly value eco-friendly options. Look for recyclable LLDPE films or bio-based alternatives (e.g., plant-derived polyethylene) to reduce your carbon footprint .

  5. Stay Compliant: For industries like food, pharmaceutical, or medical supplies, choose stretch wrap that meets FDA or GMP standards—non-negotiable for US compliance.

By focusing on your specific load characteristics, storage environment, and US operational needs, you’ll select a stretch wrap that protects your inventory, saves money, and keeps your warehouse running smoothly.

Remember: the best stretch wrap for your US warehouse is the one that balances performance, cost, and compliance.
